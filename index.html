Motivation Response explorer Trial sensitivity Relevance

The Solomon and Keller labs study how stimulation changes human brain circuits, with the aim of making neuromodulation more precise and personal. Before interpreting a person's evoked response, one practical question is worth asking: would different groups of pulses give us the same answer?

I built a small, reproducible response-review tool using an external public TMS-EEG dataset. It compares disjoint groups of trials, separates waveform shape from amplitude, and identifies estimates that remain sensitive to the number of pulses included.

How it works

For each of 22 participants, two non-overlapping sets of retained trials form two average responses. The analysis repeats this 100 times at five trial budgets. Agreement is assessed at three electrodes and two fixed latency windows. These are already processed recordings from the Aalborg cohort of Couto et al., not data collected by the Solomon or Keller labs.

Why this matters for the lab

Personalized stimulation needs measurements that are interpretable at the individual level. This analysis asks a narrower prerequisite question: how much does the response estimate depend on which trials were averaged? It is an inferred research-software opportunity aligned with the labs' research programme, not a claim about an internal bottleneck.

A useful follow-up would repeat selected readouts in a separately acquired session, with verified pulse timing and coil/target metadata. Improved within-session agreement but poor between-session agreement would motivate checking session and positioning effects. Good agreement in both would still require sham or sensory controls before attributing the response to direct neural stimulation.

Methods note

This is a reanalysis of published processed data, not an independent validation of the source paper or a reproduction of its group task-related component analysis (gTRCA). C3, Cz and Pz; 20–60 ms and 80–180 ms; budgets of 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 trials per set; and 100 seeded splits were fixed before inspecting results. Trials do not overlap within a comparison; they recur across resampling iterations. Participants, not trials or resamples, are the independent cohort units.

Measurements, missing metadata and reuse

Shape agreement is Pearson's correlation within the selected window. Amplitude discrepancy is 200 × |RMSA − RMSB| / (RMSA + RMSB), in percent. Zero-variance correlations and zero-denominator amplitude comparisons remain missing. The 10th–90th percentiles describe the seeded resampling distribution; they are not inferential confidence intervals. Correlation ignores scale, so both measurements are needed.

The source authors performed artifact processing, filtering, baseline correction and interpolation. This workflow does not repeat raw-signal cleaning. Neither window is guaranteed to isolate a neural component; stable sensory or muscle contamination can also be repeatable. The deposit does not provide sham labels, acquisition timestamps, behavioral state or tracked coil poses. Its ordinal event values must not be interpreted as real pulse timing.

The accompanying Python workflow checks file identity, channels, units, dimensions and trial coverage, and exports the same derived schema for reuse. A bounded adapter exercises the unmodified NumPy serialization helpers from NaviNIBS, pinned at 094f86d. Missing acquisition timestamps prevent creating an authentic navigation sample; the adapter reports that limitation instead of inventing a session. No neuronavigation, GUI or hardware control is performed.

Reuse would require an authorized epochs dataset, verified preprocessing history and event timing, appropriate stimulation/control labels, and independent testing with the intended acquisition system.
